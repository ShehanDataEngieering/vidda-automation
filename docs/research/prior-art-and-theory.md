# Prior Art and Theory — Vidda

Research pass on prior art (commercial competitors, open-source analogs) and primary-source theory underpinning Vidda's approach to AI-driven AMLR compliance training: free-text job description → risk-dimension extraction → RAG-grounded article mapping → progressive 4-quarter curriculum → human approval → immutable audit trail.

Compiled 2026-09-05.

---

## 1. Similar/Competing Products

### Skill Studio AI
**What it does:** Markets itself as an "AI-native agentic platform" that automatically converts regulatory documents and SOPs into verified, auditable e-learning courses — LLM-driven document analysis extracts learning objectives, generates video content, quizzes, and role-play scenarios, and auto-updates courses when the underlying regulation changes. Positions the whole thing as a "policy-to-training lifecycle."
**Vs. Vidda:** This is the closest commercial analog to Vidda's end goal (regulation → auditable training), but its own marketing describes course generation from documents directly, not a RAG pipeline with retrieval/citation grounding over the source text, nor a risk-matrix/job-description-driven gap analysis — Vidda's differentiator is starting from a *role* (free-text job description) rather than a document, and grounding every generated obligation in a specific AMLR article via retrieval rather than document-to-course summarization.
Source: [Skill Studio AI — Compliance Training LMS](https://www.skillstudio.ai/compliance-training/compliance-training-lms-ai-native-platform-for-regulated-industries) (claims: *"automatically converts regulatory documents into verified, auditable e-learning courses...providing continuous proof-of-compliance"*).

### VinciWorks — Conversational Learning
**What it does:** AI-driven "Conversational Learning" replaces static e-learning modules with adaptive, dialogue-based training (built on Google Gemini) that checks comprehension in real time and adjusts to the learner, covering AML, GDPR, anti-bribery, sanctions, and other compliance topics; delivered as SCORM packages into existing LMSs.
**Vs. Vidda:** Focused on the *delivery/engagement* layer of training (conversational quizzing per employee) rather than the *needs-analysis* layer (mapping a role to specific regulatory gaps). It has no stated RAG-over-regulation grounding, no risk-matrix generation from a job description, and no progressive quarter-by-quarter curriculum structure — it is a single adaptive course per topic, not a generated multi-quarter plan.
Source: [VinciWorks — AI Conversational Learning](https://vinciworks.com/products/ai-conversational-learning/) (claims: *"Conversational Learning replaces rote memorisation with a genuine back and forth...the AI adapts in real time to each user's knowledge and responses"*).

### AscentAI (Ascent RegTech)
**What it does:** Uses ML/NLP to scan thousands of regulatory sources and automatically extract and structure individual "obligations" from raw regulatory text, mapping them to a firm's internal policies/controls, with human compliance experts verifying the extracted data.
**Vs. Vidda:** This is the closest structural analog to Vidda's "map gaps to specific AMLR articles" step — both extract discrete obligations from regulatory text and route them to a human for verification. However, AscentAI's own page does not mention RAG, retrieval, or hallucination mitigation as a named technique (it relies on proprietary NLP/ML models plus human review, not retrieval-augmented LLM generation), and it targets policy/control mapping generally rather than *training-curriculum* generation specifically.
Source: [Ascent RegTech — AscentAI](https://www.ascentregtech.com/our-difference/ascentai/).

### Corlytics
**What it does:** A regulatory-intelligence platform (Find/Understand/Implement/Evidence regulations) that pairs each regulatory alert with curated professional-development content — e-learning modules, webinars, CPD credits — generated/aggregated via in-house AI models trained over ~5 years to reach near-99% extraction accuracy (with lawyer-in-the-loop retraining).
**Vs. Vidda:** Similar end-to-end ambition (regulation change → targeted learning content) but built on custom-trained proprietary NLP models refined over years by legal experts, rather than RAG-plus-general-purpose-LLM grounding — a materially different (much higher fixed cost, longer lead-time) bet than Vidda's "ground a general LLM via retrieval" approach.
Sources: [Corlytics — Regulatory Monitoring](https://www.corlytics.com/solutions/regulatory-monitoring/); [Digital Reasoning blog on Corlytics regulatory-mapping model training](https://digitalreasoning.com/blog/with-the-largest-offering-of-ai-powered-training-models-digital-reasoning-and-corlytics-lead-market-in-regulatory-mapping-capabilities/).

### Disprz (AI LMS with compliance-gap detection)
**What it does:** An AI-native LMS that automates compliance-learning delivery and completion tracking, and markets AI features for "identifying potential compliance gaps across the organization."
**Vs. Vidda:** Represents the "LMS vendor bolting on AI" category rather than the "RAG-grounded regulatory mapping" category — gap detection here is a marketing claim without published methodology (no stated retrieval pipeline, citation grounding, or audit-trail architecture), whereas Vidda's differentiator is making the AI's regulatory reasoning inspectable end-to-end.
Source: [Disprz — Compliance Training LMS Guide](https://disprz.ai/blog/compliance-training-lms-ultimate-guide) (vendor's own site).

**Not found:** No vendor was found that explicitly combines (a) free-text job-description intake, (b) RAG over a specific regulation's primary legal text with article-level citations, and (c) a progressive non-repeating multi-quarter curriculum, in one product — the closest matches split across the products above (Skill Studio AI for document→course, AscentAI for obligation-extraction, VinciWorks for adaptive delivery). This combination appears to be a genuine white space rather than an already-crowded category.

## 2. Open-Source Repos

### (a) RAG-over-legal/regulatory text with citation grounding and anti-hallucination framing

- **[Bhardwaj-Saurabh/ConformAI](https://github.com/Bhardwaj-Saurabh/ConformAI)** — "RAG-based compliance intelligence for AI systems" that retrieves authoritative regulatory sources (EU AI Act, GDPR, EDPB/EDPS guidance, ingested from EUR-Lex) and generates grounded, explainable, citation-enforced answers, using **article-level chunking** and **hybrid search (semantic + BM25)**. *Relevance to Vidda:* structurally the single closest OSS analog found — same regulatory domain (EU law), same chunking granularity (per-article, matching how Vidda cites specific AMLR articles), and the same hybrid-search + citation-enforcement pattern Vidda's RAG pipeline uses.
- **[lawglance/lawglance](https://github.com/lawglance/lawglance)** — "A free open source RAG based AI legal assistant" using an agentic LangGraph retrieval loop that only produces a final answer once it has retrieved enough grounding context, always with citations attached. *Relevance:* parallels Vidda's "don't answer without grounding" gate before generating AMLR-mapped content.
- **[sougaaat/RAG-based-Legal-Assistant](https://github.com/sougaaat/RAG-based-Legal-Assistant)** — Legal chatbot combining BM25 keyword search with semantic vector search, explicitly fused with **Reciprocal Rank Fusion**, plus multi-hop/multi-query retrieval, before passing context to the LLM. *Relevance:* this repo implements almost exactly Vidda's retrieval fusion strategy (BM25 + dense + RRF) but applied to general legal Q&A rather than a single regulation — useful as a reference implementation of the same fusion technique. (See also section (b).)

### (b) Hybrid BM25 + dense vector retrieval fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, with pgvector

- **[pgvector/pgvector-python](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector-python)**, specifically [`examples/hybrid_search/rrf.py`](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector-python/blob/master/examples/hybrid_search/rrf.py) — the **official pgvector example** combining Postgres full-text search (`plainto_tsquery`/`ts_rank_cd`) with pgvector dense similarity search, merged via RRF computed directly in SQL (`1.0/(k+semantic_rank) + 1.0/(k+keyword_rank)`, `k=60`) over a `FULL OUTER JOIN` of the two rankings. *Relevance:* this is the canonical, vendor-maintained reference implementation of exactly Vidda's retrieval-fusion step (BM25 + pgvector cosine + RRF), on the same database (Postgres/pgvector) Vidda uses.
- **[sougaaat/RAG-based-Legal-Assistant](https://github.com/sougaaat/RAG-based-Legal-Assistant)** — see (a) above; uses FAISS rather than pgvector but the RRF fusion logic is the same pattern.
- **[carloodq/rrf](https://github.com/carloodq/rrf)** — a minimal, standalone "reciprocal rank fusion with bm25 and semantic search" reference implementation, useful as a from-scratch worked example of the algorithm outside any specific vector-DB stack.

### (c) Structured, progressive multi-stage curriculum/training-plan generation from LLMs

- **[arun3676/ai-learning-path-generator](https://github.com/arun3676/ai-learning-path-generator)** — Combines RAG (ChromaDB) with LLM generation to produce a personalized learning path validated against a `LearningPath` Pydantic schema, broken into sequenced milestones with estimated hours, skills gained, and resources per stage. *Relevance:* closest OSS match found for "LLM outputs a structured, staged, schema-validated curriculum" — the mechanism (schema-constrained progressive milestones) parallels Vidda's Q1→Q4 non-repeating module structure, though this project targets individual self-directed learners/career paths rather than a compliance-officer-approved, role-based, organization-wide plan.
- **Gap:** No open-source project was found that specifically generates a multi-quarter, non-repeating, *regulatory-compliance* training curriculum with an explicit awareness→mastery progression and a human-approval gate. The closest analogs are either (i) generic personalized-learning-path generators like the one above (no regulatory grounding, no approval workflow), or (ii) curriculum-learning tools for *training ML models themselves* (e.g., `omarkamali/curriculus`, for scheduling datasets during LLM pretraining) — structurally unrelated despite the similar name, since they schedule training *data* for a model rather than generating a training *plan* for humans. Worth stating plainly: this specific combination (progressive human curriculum + regulatory grounding + approval gate) does not appear to have prior open-source implementation.

### (d) Immutable audit-trail / event-log patterns for AI-generated decisions

- **[AiAgentKarl/agent-audit-trail-mcp](https://github.com/AiAgentKarl/agent-audit-trail-mcp)** — An MCP server providing "immutable audit logging for AI agents": each entry embeds the SHA-256 hash of the prior entry (hash-chained, tamper-evident), with a `verify_integrity` tool to detect any alteration, and exportable reports framed explicitly around **EU AI Act Article 12** record-keeping obligations. *Relevance:* directly parallels Vidda's audit-trail requirement (AI generation, human overrides, and approvals all logged immutably) and is framed around the same "inspection-ready" regulator posture Vidda targets, just generalized to any AI agent rather than compliance-training specifically.
- **[M-DEV-1/agent-audit-log](https://github.com/M-DEV-1/agent-audit-log)** — Every agent change is captured as a hashed "Agent Trace" anchored to a public ledger (Solana Devnet) so third parties can verify a change history without trusting logs/screenshots. *Relevance:* a more exotic (blockchain-anchored) take on the same tamper-evidence goal — useful as a contrast point (Vidda's Postgres-based immutable log vs. externally-anchored hash chains) rather than a direct architectural match.

## 3. Theory and Primary Sources

**Reciprocal Rank Fusion (original paper).** Gordon V. Cormack, Charles L. A. Clarke, Stefan Büttcher, *"Reciprocal Rank Fusion outperforms Condorcet and Individual Rank Learning Methods,"* SIGIR 2009 — the paper introducing RRF (score = Σ 1/(k + rank), k≈60), showing it beats both Condorcet Fuse and every individual learning-to-rank method tested on LETOR 3. This is the technique Vidda's retrieval fusion step is a direct implementation of. Author's own hosted copy: [cormack.uwaterloo.ca/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf](https://cormack.uwaterloo.ca/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf) (also indexed at [research.google](https://research.google/pubs/reciprocal-rank-fusion-outperforms-condorcet-and-individual-rank-learning-methods/)).

**Hybrid search (BM25 + dense vectors) best practices.**
- [pgvector](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector) (official repo) and its worked hybrid-search examples in [pgvector-python](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector-python/blob/master/examples/hybrid_search/rrf.py) — the vendor-maintained reference for combining Postgres full-text search with pgvector similarity search, matching Vidda's exact stack.
- [Supabase — Hybrid Search docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/ai/hybrid-search) — a widely-used primary vendor doc walking through the same `tsvector` + `pgvector` + RRF pattern, including the SQL for merging two CTEs (full-text, semantic) via RRF. Matters here because it documents the production-grade version of the pattern Vidda implements.

**Cross-encoder reranking.** A cross-encoder jointly encodes the query and each candidate document (rather than encoding them independently, as embedding models do), letting attention span both texts and capturing interactions embedding-only retrieval misses — this is why reranking is applied *after* an initial hybrid retrieval pass rather than instead of it. Voyage AI's own documentation and release posts are the primary sources for the specific model Vidda uses:
- [Voyage AI — Rerankers docs](https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/reranker) (vendor docs, general cross-encoder explanation and API usage).
- [Voyage AI blog — "rerank-2 and rerank-2-lite"](https://blog.voyageai.com/2024/09/30/rerank-2/) — reports rerank-2 improving accuracy over OpenAI's v3-large embeddings by an average of 13.89% and over Cohere v3 by 7.14%, with 16K-token combined query+document context.
- [Voyage AI blog — "Domain-Specific Embeddings: Finance Edition (voyage-finance-2)"](https://blog.voyageai.com/2024/06/03/domain-specific-embeddings-finance-edition-voyage-finance-2/) — reports voyage-finance-2 achieving 0.831 average NDCG@10 across 11 finance retrieval datasets vs. 0.762 for OpenAI v3-large and 0.713 for Cohere English v3, with a 32K-token context window; directly supports Vidda's stated rationale for choosing a finance-tuned embedding model over a general-purpose one for terms like "enhanced due diligence."

**Grounded generation / citation-forcing / reducing hallucination in RAG for legal or regulatory text.**
- [Anthropic — Citations (Claude Platform docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations) — the primary-source API feature for forcing Claude to ground claims in supplied source documents and return the exact supporting passages, rather than relying on prompt-engineered citation instructions; directly relevant since Vidda's pipeline also asks Claude to produce article-cited answers from retrieved AMLR text.
- [Anthropic — Reduce hallucinations (Claude Platform docs)](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) — Anthropic's own guidance on grounding responses in direct quotes, allowing uncertainty, and verifying claims via citations — the primary-source complement to Vidda's "RAG + human approval, not fine-tuning" bet.
- Academic angle: hallucination in legal RAG specifically remains an open, active research problem even with retrieval grounding — e.g. arXiv's ["Citation Grounding: Detecting and Reducing LLM Citation Hallucinations via Legal Citation Graphs"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00898), which builds a verification metric (citation precision/relevance/temporality) against a ground-truth citation graph rather than assuming RAG alone eliminates hallucination — useful caution for Vidda's own claims about grounding, since RAG reduces but does not eliminate the risk it is designed to solve.

**Audit trail / event sourcing for AI-driven decisions in regulated contexts.**
- [Martin Fowler — "Event Sourcing"](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html) — the canonical description of storing all state changes as an ordered sequence of immutable events rather than mutable current-state rows, which is the underlying pattern for Vidda's "every AI generation, human override, and approval is logged" audit trail.
- [EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 12 — Record-keeping](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689) — the official EUR-Lex text requiring high-risk AI systems to "technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs)" over their lifetime, for post-market monitoring and regulatory review; directly analogous (different regulation, same EU legislative posture) to the "inspection-ready" framing Vidda uses for its own audit trail, and a useful reference point since compliance-training-assignment AI arguably sits adjacent to this regime.
- [NIST — AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — U.S. primary-source framework naming "accountable and transparent" and "explainable and interpretable" as core trustworthy-AI characteristics, and tying algorithmic accountability to auditability of AI-driven decisions — general (non-EU, voluntary) regulator-adjacent backing for the audit-trail design choice.
- [FCA — "AI and the FCA: our approach"](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach) and the FCA's own [AI Update (PDF)](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/corporate/ai-update.pdf) — the FCA's position is that there is no dedicated AI rulebook; existing Senior Managers & Certification Regime (SM&CR) accountability, governance, and risk-control obligations already apply to AI-driven outcomes, meaning firms must be able to evidence what an AI system did and why. This is the most relevant primary UK-regulator source for Vidda's "FCA/national supervisor inspection-ready" positioning, though note it addresses AI governance in financial services generally, not compliance-training generation specifically.
- **Not found:** No FCA or EU regulator guidance was found addressing AI-generated *compliance training content* specifically (as opposed to AI-driven decisions in trading, credit, or AML detection more broadly) — Vidda's audit-trail framing is a reasonable extrapolation from general AI-governance expectations rather than something directly mandated for training-generation tools today.

**AMLR 2024/1624 itself.**
- [EUR-Lex — Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 (AMLR), official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1624/oj/eng) — the primary source Vidda's RAG pipeline retrieves from; in force from 10 July 2027, replacing fragmented national AML transposition with a single directly-applicable EU regulation.
- [EBA — "Special topic: Artificial intelligence"](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/publications/special-topic-artificial-intelligence) — the European Banking Authority's own publication confirming AI (NLP, neural networks, etc.) is already broadly deployed across EU banks for AML/CFT pattern detection and client/transaction profiling, and that most banks favor in-house AI development to retain control over compliance-critical components. Notably, this publication does **not** address explainability or auditability requirements for AI in AML specifically — that gap is filled better by the general AI Act/FCA/NIST sources above than by AML-specific regulator guidance, which does not yet appear to exist in detail.

## Summary of gaps (stated explicitly per research standards)

- No commercial vendor was found combining free-text-job-description intake + RAG-grounded article citation + progressive multi-quarter curriculum + immutable audit trail in one product — this combination looks like genuine white space, assembled from parts that exist separately across Skill Studio AI, AscentAI, and VinciWorks.
- No open-source repo generates a *regulatory-compliance* progressive curriculum specifically; the closest analog (`arun3676/ai-learning-path-generator`) is a general career-learning-path generator with no regulatory grounding or approval workflow.
- No AML-specific (as opposed to general financial-services-AI) regulator guidance was found on explainability/auditability requirements — EBA's own AI publication does not cover this; the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and FCA's general AI approach are the best available proxies.
